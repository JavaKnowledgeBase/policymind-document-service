Citizens Interview Talking Points: AI Models and Agentic AI

Natural Spoken Answer

When I've used AI models, I've mostly approached them as part of a larger application workflow, not just as a chatbot or standalone model. In my recent work, especially with PolicyMind, I used AI together with document ingestion, chunking, embeddings, semantic retrieval, and response generation so the output was grounded in actual source material. For me, the important part was making it useful and reliable in a real system, so I focused a lot on secure APIs, traceability, async processing, caching, and overall production behavior.

My understanding of agentic AI is similar. I see it as more than just prompting a model well. It's really about building a system around the model so it can take steps in a controlled way, use tools, work through a task, and still stay within guardrails. That means thinking about orchestration, approvals, memory, logging, observability, and safe failure handling.

I'd say my hands-on experience today is strongest in RAG, orchestration, and AI-powered backend workflows. I wouldn't overclaim that I've built a huge autonomous agent platform end to end in an enterprise bank setting, but I do think I've worked on the core building blocks that translate directly into that kind of work. That's one of the reasons this role is exciting to me.

Shorter Spoken Version

I've used AI models mainly as part of real application workflows, especially around RAG, document intelligence, and orchestration. My focus has been on making them reliable, secure, and grounded, not just generating output. When I think about agentic AI, I think about the whole system around the model: tool use, multi-step execution, guardrails, observability, and safe behavior. My direct experience is strongest in AI workflow orchestration, and I see that as a strong foundation for building enterprise agentic systems.
